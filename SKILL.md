# Brand messaging assets
Create a small, coherent messaging kit usable across selected marketing surfaces. Preserve the user's existing voice and edit scope instead of inventing a brand persona.

## Start with a light business intake
Read the user's supplied brief and already permitted relevant project context before asking questions. Record each reused fact with its source path/URL/message, date when known, and status: supplied fact, observed evidence, assumption, or missing. Do not silently reuse another client's private information. Ask only for material gaps, ideally one compact batch: offer and objective; audience and buying situation; channel/publisher; timing; available people/assets and budget ceiling; evidence and constraints. If noncritical details are missing, produce a provisional draft with explicit assumptions.

For publisher identity, distinguish brand/company account, founder or employee personal account, and partner/customer/creator account. Use only relevant roles; name the proposed owner and approval requirement. Never attribute an experience, endorsement, opinion, or customer quote to a real person without evidence. An agent's proposed copy is not that person's approval.

## Boundaries
This skill prepares local drafts. It does not publish, schedule posts, contact anyone, buy ads, spend budgets, create accounts, or change remote repositories. Explicit requests for those actions need separate tools and authorization. Do not invent testimonials, revenue, audience size, experiment results, performance guarantees, or scarcity. Label targets and forecasts as proposed, not observed. Preserve uncertainty and identify evidence needed to approve unsupported claims. No credentials or paid services are required.

## Workflow
1. Identify whether the user needs a new kit, refinement of an existing voice, or one requested asset. For a small edit, return that edit and necessary proof notes rather than forcing the whole kit.
2. Establish the intended audience, category, problem, actual capabilities, credible difference, alternatives, desired action, and reasons to believe. Mark unvalidated differentiation as a hypothesis. Distinguish internal positioning from publishable claims.
3. Build a claim ledger with claim, source/date, supported wording, restrictions, and owner to confirm. Unsupported superlatives, performance numbers, implied guarantees, and invented quotes must not enter publishable copy. Supply a safer factual alternative and the evidence needed to unlock the stronger claim.
4. Read [messaging adaptation](references/messaging-method.md). Draft a one-sentence positioning statement, a short value proposition, and two or three distinct message pillars, each tied to a feature, customer benefit, and proof or proof gap.
5. Derive a compact voice card from approved samples: formality, energy, vocabulary, sentence style, and do/don't examples. Without samples, label the voice provisional. Adapt tone for brand, founder/employee, or partner/customer publishing roles without inventing personal experiences or endorsements.
6. Produce only useful assets: by default two headline options, one short description, one longer description, one hero/subhead/CTA combination, and one selected channel draft. Match asset length to destination constraints; verify current platform rules if a hard limit matters. Explain tradeoffs between headline options without claiming either will perform better.
7. If bilingual output is requested, localize intent and CTA naturally. Keep product names and factual qualifications consistent across languages; do not add new claims in translation.
8. Deliver the [messaging kit](templates/messaging-kit.md), claim ledger, and approval questions. Use the [fictional example](examples/fictional-brand.md) for structure only.

## Completion check
Check consistency across assets, unsupported or overbroad claims, persona authenticity, meaningful CTA destination, and preservation of user voice. If evidence is weak, deliver a conservative draft with visible gaps. No content is published.
